This is a data and AI course, not a technology tour. Larkfield is a team that decided to build on public chain data, and everything that happened to them afterwards happened to their pipeline. The upside is real and it is not the one in the marketing: no access negotiation, no SLA, no gatekeeper, full history available to anyone. The bill arrives in three places. The unit of identity is an address rather than a person, so a count of about 1,000 active addresses stands for 304 real parties and the standard cleanup only gets you to 696. Some of that population is manufactured on purpose by people paid to move the number. And the source can withdraw what it already told you, which leaves 511 rows of your warehouse describing history that no longer exists, with every check green. Six leader sessions on the decision, ten practitioner sessions on the build, and two benches that run in the page rather than quoting a table.

What is real here

Every number in this course was computed before any page quoted it. The world is generated on purpose so that the truth is knowable; the algorithms run over it are the ordinary ones.

The clusteringA real union-find over real co-spend links, scored against a truth of 304 parties. Across 1,365 settings, the number of clusters holding two real parties is 0
The warehouseA loader running over a chain that reorganises. Measured over 4 seeds, 5 depths and every lag: about 169 wrong rows per block of depth minus lag
The engine checks itselfFive properties on every page load. The fourth exists because the first three all passed against a fault, and session 9 is the record of it
